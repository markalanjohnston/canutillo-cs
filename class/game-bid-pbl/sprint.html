Paper is cheap. That’s the whole strategy. Every problem your crew finds today costs a pencil. The same problem on Bid Day costs the contract. So this week you finish every screen, then run a professional’s move on your own work: the audit. Un error hoy cuesta un lápiz; el día de la propuesta, el contrato.

Step 1 — The screen list (with your crew)

Agree it out loud before anyone draws: 3 to 5 screens, one job each, every member owns at least one. Then each of you types the list in Classroom, with a star on YOUR screen.

  1. Start and Play and Win/End are the spine — the client must walk that path.
  2. Setup earns a screen only if the PLAYER acts — place your ships, pick your character. Automatic setup (shuffling, dealing) dies into a tap.
  3. A screen with no job is decoration. “Settings” with nothing to set, a Rules screen (the rulebook died — teach it inside Play), a splash page that repeats Start: cut them.

De 3 a 5 pantallas, un trabajo cada una, cada miembro dibuja al menos una.

Step 2 — Wireframe every screen

Same rules as before, no exceptions: boxes and labels ONLY (ugly on purpose) · every tappable thing marked with its label · one screen, one job, written under the frame · your map feeds the screens. Cajas y etiquetas; cada tap marcado; un trabajo por pantalla.

Step 3 — The Fence Audit (the professional’s move)

Lay the screens in walk order. A different crew member reads each row aloud. Point at the evidence on your frames BEFORE you mark PASS or FIX. A FIX is a WIN — you caught it while it was cheap. Fix it on paper now, initial the row.

#The ten rows
1Count: at least 3 screens, at most 5.
2Portrait: phone-shaped, tall, one-thumb playable.
3Recognizable: a fan names your game from the Play screen alone, in ten seconds.
4Original: nothing traced or copied — icons drawn your way.
5Families: a 9-year-old could read every label.
6No money: no ads, prices, or coin shops.
7Slides can fake it: nothing Slides can’t fake with a click — no dice physics, typing, or video.
8Every tappable thing labeled, on every screen.
9One screen, one job, written under each frame.
10The path: Start → Play → Win, reachable by taps alone.

An audit that finds nothing found nothing. Una auditoría que no encuentra nada, no encontró nada.

Step 4 — The full walk, then submit

Trade complete sets with another crew. The reader walks Start → Play → Win with a finger, narrating what each tap does. The designers stay SILENT. Initials on every screen that survives; a failed screen gets its labels fixed — not its designer defended — and is re-read.

Then photograph every screen AND the audit sheet and attach them in Classroom. Keep the paper frames — next lesson they become your Slides: every box a slide, every tap a link. Fotos de todo; guarda los papeles — la próxima lección se convierten en tus Slides.
