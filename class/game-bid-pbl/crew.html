You didn’t pick your crew — that’s the job. In industry you rarely choose your team; the skill is making THIS one work. Your crew signed a contract before the project started, because accountability only exists when the terms were agreed ahead of time. La responsabilidad nace de acuerdos hechos ANTES de empezar.

What your contract holds

  1. Three norms — each one passes the test: could a crewmate actually call you on it? “We work hard” fails. “We trade roles each class” passes.
  2. Division of labor — your crew defined equitable as equal PARTS or equal EFFORT. Either is fine. A name next to nothing is not.
  3. Accountability terms — what happens first when someone misses an agreed term. Decided calmly in advance, not invented mid-argument.
  4. Your own conflict plan (Section 5, in YOUR words) — disagreement starts inside the crew. Mr. Johnston is the escalation, not the opening move.

The six words your crew runs on

Collaboration · colaboración
One shared product — everyone’s fingerprints on it.
Division of labor · división del trabajo
Every job has a name next to it.
Norms · acuerdos
Working agreements set BEFORE the work. Specific beats noble.
Consensus · consenso
Everyone can live with it, and says so out loud.
Accountability · responsabilidad
Answering to the terms you agreed to.
Conflict resolution · resolución de conflictos
Crews that survive argue about the WORK, not each other — and they try their own plan first.
When your crew hits trouble, run your Section 5:
First I will… (talk to the crewmate, name the norm, re-split the work)
If that doesn’t fix it, together we will…
We bring it to Mr. Johnston only after…
Joined late or missed the contract day? You join an existing crew: read their agreed terms, then write your own Section 5 fresh — never copied. Ask Mr. Johnston which crew is yours. Si llegaste tarde: lee los acuerdos de tu equipo y escribe tu propia sección 5.
