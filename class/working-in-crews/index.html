Working in Crews 🛠

Fundamentals of Computer Science · Mr. Johnston · complete today, at your own pace, in order

Why today matters: this week you start a design project in a crew of 2–3, bidding for a real contract. Professional crews don’t survive on talent... they survive on agreements. Today you build the vocabulary and the thinking your crew will run on. Work alone or buddy up with ONE neighbor if you don’t have a device.

1 · Bell work 5 min

On your paper packet, side 1, top box: Think of the best team you have EVER been part of... sports, games, band, family, anything. What made it WORK? 2-3 complete sentences.

2 · The vocabulary of teams 15 min

Read all six. Then, in your notebook or on the paper packet, write each term in your own words — your definition, not ours.
Collaboration · colaboración
Working on ONE shared thing together — everyone’s fingerprints on the same product.
Division of labor · división del trabajo
Splitting the job on purpose: who does what, decided out loud, not by whoever grabs it first.
Norms · acuerdos
The written rules a team agrees to BEFORE trouble starts. Specific beats noble.
Consensus · consenso
A decision everyone can live with — not everyone’s favorite, but nobody’s veto.
Accountability · responsabilidad
Owning your piece so your crew never has to chase you for it.
Conflict resolution · resolución de conflictos
The plan for disagreement. Good crews argue about the WORK, not about each other.

3 · Watch OR read 10 min

Option A (video): search YouTube for a short video (under 6 minutes) on what makes teams work or group norms for students. Headphones or muted with captions.
Option B (read): re-read the six cards above and the scenarios on the paper packet.
Either way, capture two ideas worth stealing for your own crew — you’ll use them in Task 4.

4 · Light research + write 15 min

Find one real tip for handling disagreement in a team — from your video, an article, or a site you trust. Use your device for the research if you have one (buddy up if you don’t). Then write, on your paper packet:
  1. Three norms you would want in your crew — specific ones. (“We trade roles each class” counts. “We work hard” does not.)
  2. Your disagreement tip, in your own words, and where you found it.
  3. One sentence: which of the six vocabulary words will be hardest for you personally, and why. Honesty scores here.
Turn in your PAPER PACKET to the substitute before you leave — finished or not, name on it. (Nothing is in Google Classroom today... everything lives on paper.) Finished early? Add a fourth norm for handling a crewmate who is absent — that one WILL come up.
Everyone turns in the paper packet — it is the record of today’s work, and you get it back to finish later. Devices are for the reading, the video, and the research; the writing lives on paper today. Nothing today is busy work: every piece of this comes back when crews form.
